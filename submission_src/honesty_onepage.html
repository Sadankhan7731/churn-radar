What the system gets wrong

  • About two in three people on the call list will not actually leave (32.8% hit rate). Offers must be cheap enough for that to be affordable.
  • Everything is assumed data. I wrote every signal's effect, the release calendar, the live demo database and its market shift. The work proves the method, not that these signals matter at a real company.
  • Reasons are associations, not causes. One true rule (support tickets) was not recovered because of collinearity. The what-if simulator's effects assume risk falls as if a signal changed; that needs an experiment to prove.
  • The checks are strong but not perfect. Code cannot judge meaning; the judge is itself a language model and can be wrong or unavailable. Sentences that cannot be verified are shown with a red flag rather than hidden.
  • The learning loop is demonstrated on simulated data. Right after retraining, accuracy is measured on the data the model learned from (labelled in the app); a fresh check needs 30 days of outcomes.
  • Other companies' uploads are scored with a model trained on Lumen Play's patterns, so their scores are indicative until retrained on their own outcomes.

What I would not yet put in front of a customer

  • Nothing goes to customers automatically, by design. The tool recommends; a manager approves each outreach and each model switch (auto-promote is off).
  • No use on real subscribers until retrained and re-evaluated on real company data, inside the company, with the same held-out test and ceiling check.
  • No spending decisions from the simulator alone. Save rates and uptake are assumptions; an A/B test with a holdout group is needed (and would enable uplift and survival models).
  • The live connector stays inside the company network. It has no sign-in and binds to the local machine; exposing it would break the Phase 1 rule that customer rows never leave the network.

What it costs

  • Weekly briefing: about USD 0.001 per memo on average, including the judge and any repair (about PKR 0.30); a few cents a year at one a week. Assistant: about USD 0.0002–0.0006 per question. Learning loop: no LLM cost.
  • Hosting: Vercel free tier. LLM: Groq free tier, whose limits (8,000 tokens a minute, 200,000 a day per model) are the real constraint; daily team use would need a paid tier, still cents a day.
  • The real cost is the campaign: people's time and discounts. With default assumptions the simulator shows a net monthly benefit for top-10% targeting versus a loss for random targeting.
