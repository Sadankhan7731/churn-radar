      Methodology

      What the system does on each run, and where it falls short

      Process

      1. Score. A logistic regression estimates each subscriber's probability of cancelling within 30 days.
      2. Explain. Each score is decomposed into its three strongest reasons, each tied to an observed value.
      3. Aggregate. Totals and shares are reduced to thirteen numbered facts. A privacy control rejects any identifier.
      4. Draft. gpt-oss-120b on Groq drafts the memorandum and must cite a fact in every sentence.
      5. Verify numbers. Code confirms each citation exists and each figure appears in the cited fact.
      6. Review meaning. An independent model, qwen3.8-27b, judges whether each sentence is faithful to its facts.
      7. Repair. Flagged statements are returned to the drafting model once, with the reasons.
      8. Decide. The retention team chooses whom to contact. Nothing is sent to subscribers automatically.

      Known limitations

      1. Two of the three Phase 1 model targets were missed. Even a perfect model could not reach 40% top-decile precision on this data.
      2. The data is synthetic. Real behaviour is less tidy; the model must be re-validated on real data inside the company before any use.
      3. The semantic reviewer is itself a language model and can err. The numerical check is strict but cannot judge meaning.
      4. Groq processes requests outside Pakistan. This is acceptable only because aggregated, non-identifying facts are sent.
